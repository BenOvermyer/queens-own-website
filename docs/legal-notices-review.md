# Legal notices draft review

The standalone draft at `/legal-notices/` supersedes the original club page’s legal text. Front matter identifies it as a draft; no effective date or claim of legal approval is assigned. The old fragment remains as a link to the new page, and source reimports also remove the superseded text.

Before adoption, confirm:

- The club’s responsible entity and contacts, current authorization from Mercedes Lackey, and the scope of any fan-club or publication rules. The draft avoids asserting tax-exempt status, incorporation, or ongoing author supervision without supporting records.
- Which works the club owns or has permission to distribute, including whether the original household-copy permission can be honored for each publication. The draft records that earlier statement without expanding it into a blanket license.
- Artwork and book-cover provenance, missing credits, and actual display permissions. The original assertions that uncredited artwork is public domain and that Amazon authorized covers are not carried forward without evidence. Rewriting the notice does not resolve underlying permission gaps.
- Any specific licenses on individual contributions and the permissions for underlying fictional material. Do not apply a site-wide Creative Commons license by inference from the old notice.
- Submission terms covering publication, editing, attribution, and contributor rights. The draft does not create a copyright transfer or assume that browsing or submission forms a contract.
- Applicable jurisdiction and whether formal copyright-complaint procedures or additional notices are needed. The contact section is an ordinary reporting channel, not a claim of DMCA registration or safe-harbor compliance.

References: the [U.S. Copyright Office’s copyright overview](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) describes ownership, licensing, and exceptions. Its [permission guide](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/m10.pdf) discusses obtaining permission from rights holders. The [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/index.html) explains license scope and the need to control the rights being licensed. These references inform the draft without determining the club’s jurisdiction or verifying any existing permissions.

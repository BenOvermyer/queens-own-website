<font face="Harem">
<h2>Cantrips</h2>
</font>
</p><p>
Cantrips are useful little spells that apprentices learn when they begin
to work with magic.  These are illusions that are particularly entertaining
for children.
</p><p>
</p><table border="2" cellpadding="2" cellspacing="2" width="100%">
<tr>
<th align="center" colspan="2" valign="center"><h2>Rainbow Swirl</h2></th>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Effect:</th>
<td>Colors swirl in a bowl of white milk.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Ingredients:</th>
<td><ul>
<li>1 cup milk
</li><li>Five drops food coloring, various colors
</li><li>Liquid diswashing soap
</li></ul></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Method:</th>
<td><ol>
<li>Put the milk in a bowl.
</li><li>Have an audience member drop the food coloring onto the milk.  Spread the
drops out.  Don't put them all in the same place.
</li><li>Drop the liquid soap one drop at a time to the milk and food coloring.
</li></ol></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Explanation:</th>
<td>As the soap causes the surface tension of the milk to decrease, the colors
are able to swirl together.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Credit:</th>
<td>You can find this trick explained in the October 1998 issue of <a href="http://www.parents.com/cgi-bin/WebObjects/Parents/PM">Parents Magazine</a>, p. 134.</td>
</tr>
</table>
<p>
</p><table border="2" cellpadding="2" cellspacing="2" width="100%">
<tr>
<th align="center" colspan="2" valign="center"><h2>Magic Potions</h2></th>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Effect:</th>
<td>Potions change colors as ingredients are added.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Ingredients:</th>
<td><ul>
<li>1 head purple cabbage
</li><li>Hot water
</li><li>Lemon juice
</li><li>Vinegar
</li><li>Baking soda
</li><li>Milk
</li><li>Tea
</li></ul>
<p>
You will also need:
</p><p>
</p><ul>
<li>1 kettle
</li><li>1 strainer
</li><li>1 pitcher
</li><li>6 jars
</li></ul></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Method:</th>
<td><ol>
<li>Coarsely chop the cabbage.
</li><li>Soak the cabbage in the kettle in very hot water for 1 hour.
</li><li>Strain the liquid from the cabbage into the pitcher.
</li><li>Line up the six jars and fill them with the liquid from the pitcher.
</li><li>Beginning with the second jar, drop the lemon juice, vinegar,
baking soda, milk and tea into the jars, one new ingredient for each jar.
</li></ol></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Explanation:</th>
<td>Just as a litmus test paper changes color to detect acidity, so does the
cabbage juice.  Acid ingredients cause the potion to change pink.  Basic
ingredients cause the potion to change blue or green.  How acidic or basic
the ingredient is and how much you add determines how much the color changes.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<th align="left" valign="top">Credit:</th>
<td>You can find this trick explained in the October 1998 issue of <a href="http://www.parents.com/cgi-bin/WebObjects/Parents/PM">Parents Magazine</a>, p. 134.</td>
</tr>
</table>

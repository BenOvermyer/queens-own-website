<img align="right" alt="QO Logo" border="0" loading="lazy" src="/qo180.jpg"/>Dear Misty Fans,
</p><p>
The four chapters of <i>Bardic Voices: The LArk &amp; the Wren</i> that many of you received in the mail is called a teaser book.  It's a form of advertising and, in this case, a thank you from Baen to all QO members who can be reached by surface mail.  I hoped it would be viewed as a pleasant surprise.  If anyone objects to my making your address available to Baen, please let m eknow, and I will remove your name form future mailings.  Baen does not have the QO membership list; I am printing labels for them as needed.  As I said, it's Baen's way of showing their appreciation to Misty's fans.  There will be more goodies in your mailboxes from time to time.
</p><p>
<i>Jinx High</i> is finally on shelves in NJ, as is <i>Winds of Fate</i>.  Wow!  Two new books by Misty to read!  Yes, the George Louvis mentioned in <i>Jinx High</i> is a relative--my older son to be exact.  He's a nusician but he's never had an experience like that prom gig.
</p><p>
If you feel you can't afford a hardcover book, nag your local library to buy <i>Winds of Fate</i>, take up a collection among your friends and buy a to-be-shared copy, bet it from a friend, put it on your Christmas list (and ask for it now)--you've got to read this one!  Heart-stopping moments, twists and turns in the plot, characters so well drawn they feel like old friends . . . <i>Winds of Fate</i> is probably Misty's best work to date.
</p><p>
<i>The Elvenbane</i>, Misty's collaboration with Andre Norton, is the featured selection of the Science Fiction Book Club for November.  It's being offered at a "limited time only" price [Ed. note 2003:  Out-of-date information withheld.]. If you don't belong to the SFBC, now is a good time to join.  You get six books for [Ed. note 2003:  Out-of-date information withheld.], and the member friend who enlists you gets [Ed. note 2003:  Out-of-date information withheld.].  Your obligation is to [Ed. note 2003:  Out-of-date information withheld.].  I've already sent in my order, and I'll be glad to send the enrollment form to the first person who asks for it.
</p><p>
Papers have been signed on the Aerie, Misty and Larry's new home.  As Laary described it via GEnie on September 11th:  "The closing went smoothly.  To insure it would be fun for all, Misty went in a black suede skirt and blue satin and lave top, with her Aussie Digger hat, and I arrived in a white tux-and-tails.  The paperwork took about half an hour, and the jokes lasted another half hour.  The moving has begun!  And yes, for those wondering, I *did* wear my black sequined shoes." (Thanks to Michelle Lutz for acting as our GEnie reporter.)
</p><p>
Misty and Larry ahge yet to spend a night in their new home.  They signed the papers, moved their belongings, and left in their RV for Contradiction and a bit of visiting, expecting to be back home about now.  They travel with laptops and conseintiously write, write, write, even when on the road.
</p><p>
<b>STARLOG</b> magazine will feature an interview with Misty in its November issue (probably available now).  It will have photos, too.  The interview is by Brad Sinor, one of the Guests at last year's SoonerCon.  <b>STARLOG</b> is sometimes hard to track down; be persistent.
</p><p>
There's a full page ad for <i>Bardic Voices</i> in the September issue fo <b>LOCUS</b> that is headlined "A start of a new series from one of the hottest writers in fantasy today."  Also in that issue is a publicity photo of Misty and Larry.  It's lovely, but I like our informal shot better.  If you're curious about Misty and LArry's appearnace and can't find a copy of <b>LOCUS</b>, send me [Ed. note 2003:  Out-of-date information withheld.].  My expenses have been met, so all money collected now will go to S.O.A.R., one of Misty and LArry's favorite projects.

</p><h2>Personals</h2> [Heading missing in this issue of the newsletter]
<p>
Why hasn't your fan message or Herald acknowledgement appeared?  Maybe the post office lost it, or the cats ate it, or there are fifty others that arrived before it did, or maybe--I can't read it or I decided it was inappropriate.  If you write to me, especially in pencil or in tiny handwriting, and I have to puzzle out the words, the letter goes to the bottom of the pile.  Easy-to-read fan messages and Herald material always go in first.  I'm not being mean; I have vision problems.  Puzzling over illegible handwriting gives me a headache.  Too many headaches and nothing gets done.  Don't threaten me because something you sent hasn't appeared in the newsletter.  Sending me $8 for ten issues of hte newsletter does <b>not</b> buy me!  If you are unhappy with QO, I will quickly refund your money and delete your address.
</p><p>
I decide what is appropriate for the newsletter.  This is not a lonely hearts club, nor a forum for the weird and outre.  Queen's Own exists to bring you news of Misty's books and convention appearances, to open doors into other aspects of fandom, and to permit you to correspond with other readers.  Local chapters allow you to make even closer contact with fellow fans, and zines and other projects allow you to show off new skills.  If what you consider "fun" I consider to be in bad taste, you will be ignored.
</p><p>
One last bit of nagging--Stop freaking out the Mundanes!  When you attend a convention you are a representative of fandom.  It may amuse you to frighten a maid or waitress, but it certainly causes the rest of us problems.  (See Misty's <i>Chilfren of the Night</i>, pages 10-11.)  I worked very hard to convince a maid at Contradiction that we were harmless folk who just liked to dress funny and play pretend.  Remember, if the staff of a hotel decideds SF/F fans cause problems, conventions will find themselves without homes.  If you consider simple courtesy a compronise of your individuality, stay home!

Dear Misty Fans,
</p><p>
Last month we learned about some of the birst that share the "Eerie Aerie," Misty and LArry's home: a Blue-Streak Lory named Visyr; Jayde, a Yellow-Collar Mini Macaw; Harlie Quinn, a Blue-And-Gold Macaw; and Ikaika, an Umbrella Cockatoo.  There are more!
</p><p>
From Misty: "The bird that loves Larry more than anything in the world is another small bird, our Goffin's Cockatoo, Kechara.  She has the same personality as the character Kechara, as you might well imagine.  Goffin's Cockatoos are small, about the size fo a Mini-Macaw, white with peach shadings to the feathers of the head and neck, and a tiny 'shark fin' crest.  Everything is a bird-toy for Kechara: her wings, the laptop computer, your hair . . . They are quite sweet and playful, and they make the <i>strangest</i> sounds!  When Kechara is babbling, it sounds as if I have a house-full of aliens!  When people want a cockatoo but have an apartment or are not ready for a big bird, I suggest a Goffin's.
</p><p>
"The third cockatoo in the house was my Christmas present, Kirin (for the Chinese unicorn) was abandoned by his owner at my vet's office, and Dr. Paul Welch asked me if we wanted to buy him, because Paul knew I ad lost a Moluccan Cockatoo two years ago and was heartbroken.  Seabreeze had been my first big bird, and he caught a rare form of salmonella and died in thirty-six hours.  We went in to look at Kirin, and to my amazement he was almost exactly like Seabreeze; the same size, sex, personality, and the same age that Seabreeze would have been if he were still alive.  I fell in love, and LArry bought him on the spot for me.  Moluccan Cockatoos are the same general shape as Umbrellas, but they are bigger and heavier--and pink!  The crest-feathers under the top layer are a bright salmon-pink, which gives them the name 'Salmon-Crested Cockatoo.'  Both breeds come from Indonesia originally, but of course, all my birds are hand-raised and domestically bred, not wild-caught.  You should never buy a wild-caught bird; it is ilegal to import most of them anyway, and a wild-caught bird will never be able to be the loving pet that a hand-raised bird will be.  Kirin is not as cuddly as Ikaika, but he is much more playful; he is learning to play 'fetch,' and I have had him only a couple of weeks.
</p><p>
"This brings us to the 'Brains of the Outfit'--Elsie B., my African Grey, who was the Christmas present for last year and the next several years from the Wollheims at DAW.  Elsie is an Agrican Grey Parrot; although Greys are supposed to be very reserved, you would never know if from the way Elsie plays with her toys and with me.  She is very much a two-person bird, however; she seldom lets anyone but me or Larry approach her, and she has a nasty bite, worse than even Harlie's!  Elsie picks up sounds, words and phrases without any of us ever teaching them to her.  She whistles the beginning of the 'Colonel Bogie March' (<i>Bridge on the River Kwai</i>) and the beginning of the 'Star-Spangled Banner.'  She makes a sound like a gun, sneezes like Larry, says 'Wooo!' when we play with Ikaika, laughs like me, and imitates all the other sounds the other birds make, plus has a huge variety of clicks, clucks, barks, and whistles.  She says 'Sega!  Hello Elsie, Hello love, I'm sorry, Okay, Well, Up' and says ' love you, Pretty bird, Hi Baby,' in Ikaika's voice!  She is also a tattle-tale; when Harlie or Ikaika are yelling too loud, she shouts 'Kaika!' or 'Harlie!' in a voice of total disgust!
</p><p>
"If all this sounds delightful, and you can't wait to get a bird of your own, you should be aware that having an exotic bird means work and a lot of responsibility.  Exotic birds are highly intelligent adn need stimulation and attention.  By nad-raising and socializing these young birds, we are making ourselves the substitute for their flock; we must now provide all the socieal contact adn stimulation they need and crave, not just once in a while, but every day." (More about the care and feeding of birds in future newsletters . . . )

Fandom is for fun, but sometimes we learn things in fandom that are very useful in Mundania.  In the "Pet P-raed" column of the issue of <b>The Michael Praed Netweork/Newsletter</b> was a reminder that all of us who have pets should make arrangements for those pets in case of an emergency.  Prepare a release form that says something like "I (your name), the undersigned, hereby give permission to the bearer of this letter to take charge of my animals (list them all), in the event of an emergency.".  Sign it and give a copy to your designated "pet sitter" and leave one copy where it can be easily found.  If you were suddenly taken ill or injured (or worse) and the police were called in, your pets could end up in an Animal Shelter and be put to sleep before your family or friends rememebred them.
